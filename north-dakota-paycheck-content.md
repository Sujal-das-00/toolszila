### For "North Dakota State Tax Information" section:

North Dakota has one of the simplest and most taxpayer-friendly state income tax systems in the United States. The state uses a progressive tax structure with just three tax brackets, ranging from 1.9% to 2.5% — among the lowest state income tax rates nationwide.

**Key North Dakota State Tax Facts:**
- **Tax Brackets (2026):** 1.9%, 2.29%, and 2.5%
- **Standard Deduction:** Single filers: $6,850 | Married filing jointly: $13,700
- **No Local Income Taxes:** North Dakota does not allow cities or municipalities to levy additional income taxes
- **Federal Tax Deduction:** North Dakota allows you to deduct federal income tax paid from your state taxable income

For a $75,000 salary, a single filer in North Dakota pays approximately $1,608 in state income tax annually — significantly less than neighboring states like Minnesota or Montana.

### For "Social Security" section:

Social Security tax is a flat 6.2% withholding on earned income, split evenly between you and your employer. For 2026, the Social Security wage base limit is $176,100, meaning any income above this threshold is not subject to Social Security tax.

**How Social Security affects your North Dakota paycheck:**
- You pay 6.2% on wages up to $176,100
- Your employer matches this contribution
- Self-employed individuals pay both halves (12.4% total)
- On a $75,000 salary, you'll contribute $4,650 annually to Social Security

Social Security benefits may be partially taxable at the federal level depending on your combined income, but North Dakota does not tax Social Security benefits at the state level — another tax advantage for retirees.

### For "Medicare" section:

Medicare tax is 1.45% on all earned wages, with no income cap. Both employees and employers each pay 1.45%, totaling 2.9%. High earners pay an additional 0.9% Medicare surtax on wages exceeding $200,000 for single filers or $250,000 for married couples filing jointly.

**Medicare Tax Breakdown for North Dakota Workers:**
- Base rate: 1.45% on all wages
- Additional Medicare Tax: 0.9% on wages over $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (MFJ)
- Employer matches the 1.45% base rate but not the additional 0.9%
- On a $75,000 salary, you'll pay $1,087.50 annually in Medicare tax

Unlike Social Security, there's no wage base limit for Medicare — every dollar you earn is subject to this tax.

### New Section: "Frequently Asked Questions About North Dakota Paychecks"

**Q: Does North Dakota have local income taxes?**
A: No. North Dakota prohibits cities and municipalities from levying local income taxes. Your only income tax obligations are federal and state.

**Q: How does North Dakota tax retirement income?**
A: North Dakota does not tax Social Security benefits. Other retirement income, such as pension distributions and 401(k) withdrawals, is taxed at the regular state income tax rates.

**Q: Do I need to file a North Dakota state tax return?**
A: If you're a North Dakota resident with income above the federal filing threshold, you generally need to file a state return. Non-residents with North Dakota-source income may also need to file.

**Q: How often should I check my paycheck withholdings?**
A: Review your withholdings whenever you have a major life change (marriage, new job, salary increase, or new dependent) or if you owed significant taxes or received a large refund when filing your return.

**Q: Are there any North Dakota tax credits I should know about?**
A: North Dakota offers various credits including a resident tax credit for taxes paid to other states, a marriage penalty credit, and credits for certain investments. Consult a tax professional for credits that may apply to your situation.
